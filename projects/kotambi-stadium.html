schematic design lead for the north stand media block of the kotambi international cricket stadium, integrating media, commentary, and broadcast facilities across a five-level structure. the brief called for a stand that could serve press, commentators, and camera crews simultaneously, without any of those functions compromising the others' sightlines.

the eye of the stadium

the north stand serves as the heart of surveillance and communication for the area. dubbed "the eye" of the stadium, it houses the print media block, commentary boxes, camera controls, broadcast facilities, and the press conference room, stacked across five levels from ground level up to roughly 21 metres.

the design mirrors that watchful role: sleek lines and a strategic position give the stand optimal sightlines for media personnel and commentators, while reinforcing its symbolic significance as the vantage point overseeing everything unfolding on the field.

materials and form

concrete and glass carry the narrative. concrete gives the structure its foundation of resilience and reliability, while glass brings transparency and light, connecting the enclosed media functions back to the stadium and the match beyond. sweeping roof lines and angular geometries were used to suggest movement and energy, rooting a contemporary form in the wider vocabulary of cricket architecture in india.

master plans, circulation networks, and wayfinding systems were also developed for related public, residential, and commercial projects during this period, totalling over 150,000 sq ft of built space, alongside construction documentation and contractor coordination for residential towers and apartment interiors.
