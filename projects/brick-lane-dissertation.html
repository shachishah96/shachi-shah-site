interplay between place identity and affordances

environmental psychologyqualitative researchplace identity & affordance theory
site
brick lane, london
method
in-depth interviews, thematic analysis, fieldwork
degree
msc environmental psychology, merit

an inquiry into london's brick lane, asking how physical aspects of an environment support the activities and behaviours of the people who use it. place identity was first introduced as those dimensions of the self that define the individual's personal identity in relation to the physical environment. the way an environment offers possibilities for activity and perception is what the theory of affordances describes.

the question

although affordances and place identity have been studied separately, there's limited research exploring their interconnection. this study explored the relationship between the two concepts in the context of urban heritage spaces, asking: how do physical aspects of urban heritage sites support perceived affordances in such places, and what is the relationship between perceived affordances and experienced place identity?

method

qualitative research through in-depth interviews, thematic analysis, and urban fieldwork on brick lane itself, a site chosen precisely because its layered heritage, market culture, and constant redevelopment make questions of identity and use unusually visible.

why it matters to the practice

this dissertation is the theoretical backbone of a lot of what shows up later under note: the idea that a building or a street doesn't just get used, it gets read, and that reading shapes who people understand themselves to be in that place.
