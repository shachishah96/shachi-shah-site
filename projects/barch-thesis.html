interpretative capacities of built-form

urban designexhibition & installation design
site
juhu selfie point, mumbai
outcome
"a landscape of unusual objects"
degree
b.arch, cgpa 8.34

an analysis of public spaces resulted in identifying specific characteristics of built-form that enable strong associations and play a role in how people interpret space. the design proposal that came out of it was called "a landscape of unusual objects."

the site

the site of intervention is a transactional space between the juhu selfie point and an informal settlement, inviting users of diverse nature. the brief was a result of a combination of necessities and aspirations of the people who actually use that space day to day, not an abstract urban design exercise.

the proposal

the intent was to develop built-form that provides interpretative opportunities and holds programmes such as exhibition spaces, play spaces, event spaces, and intimate pockets, alongside organisational facilities for the targeted users. a series of intuitive illustrations became the base for developing narratives around each object.

the broader architectural strategy was to create a landscape of objects acting as an interface between the offered natural landscape and the built form of the city. each object holds strong associations to specific experiences, acting as an anchor for multiple activities while extending itself to create possibilities for much more. these objects act as stimulants, affording activities and imagination.

each object was developed using specific elements that symbolise a particular activity. the unusual overlap and configuration of these elements let them be imagined and occupied in different ways, connected through ancillary programmes including landscape, walking paths, and resting pavilions. the learning spaces, for example, acknowledge different ways in which "learning" happens, combining conventional seating with spaces for knowledge sharing and community interaction.
