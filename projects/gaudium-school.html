gaudium school places a strong emphasis on holistic development and environmental sustainability, built around nurturing the whole child by fostering intellectual, emotional, physical, and social growth. the landscape and circulation strategy was developed to embody those values spatially, not just programmatically.

the swale as classroom

a shallow water body, the swale, runs through the campus doing double duty: a sustainable water management system and an engaging outdoor learning environment for students. it was designed with each contour planned to serve different functions depending on where the swale meets the built form. percolation chambers and a permeable crs stone lining facilitate natural filtration and groundwater recharge while collecting rainwater runoff.

the swale's landscape design includes dry, naturalistic plantings and rough stones, creating a visually appealing space for students to explore and observe. steps and stones let students safely access the swale when it's dry, folding it into daily use as an interactive outdoor learning feature rather than a fenced-off utility.

material choices

geocell, a material used in construction and slope protection with topsoil and other infill materials, was used in place of concrete across several landscaped areas, a more sustainable approach that avoids concrete entirely on steep slopes in narrow spaces.

large open-plan classrooms encourage interactive teaching methods, and the ample shared spaces, amphitheatre, landscaped pockets, fish pond, and sitting areas, promote social interaction and a strong sense of community throughout the school day.
